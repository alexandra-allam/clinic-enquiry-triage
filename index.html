The problem

A small business contact form collects everything: serious buyers, students, people who are only curious, and spam. Someone has to read each one, decide how serious it is, and write a reply. The serious buyers wait in the same queue as everyone else.

I wanted a workflow that sorts leads on arrival, gets the best ones to a person within minutes, and drafts every other reply so it only needs a quick check before sending.

How it works
  1. 1

  2. 2
    AI qualificationThe model returns a fixed structure: segment, intent and a 1 to 10 score.
  3. 3

    Rejected tabno emails sent
  4. 4

    Known emailupdate the record and count the submission
  5. 5

    Instant alertwith a suggested reply
  6. 6

  7. 7

    Approvedemail sent to the lead
  8. 8

Separate workflowEvery Monday at 8:00, a weekly report emails lead volume, quality and approval rates.

What testing found

Most of the work was here. Each of these runs reported "success" while doing the wrong thing, so I only found them by checking the output against the source data.

  • Spam was labelled as a small business

    Found

    The segment list had no fallback, so the AI had to pick SMB or Enterprise even for spam and students. Spam would have received a nurture email.

    Fixed

    Added "Unknown" and "Invalid" options and routed invalid leads to their own tab.

  • A lead was silently dropped

    Found

    Two leads entered the approval step in one run and one came out. The approval step only handled the first item, and the run still showed success.

    Fixed

    Wrapped the draft, approval and logging steps in a loop so each lead is handled on its own.

  • The weekly report tripled the rejected count

    Found

    The sheet had 1 rejected lead and the report said 3. The second sheet-read ran once for every row from the first read.

    Fixed

    Set that step to run once, then checked every figure in the report by hand against the sheet.

  • Spam was dragging the averages down

    Found

    The report counted spam as a lead, which lowered the average score and mixed junk into the segment breakdown.

    Fixed

    Reported valid and rejected leads separately and calculated quality metrics from valid leads only.

  • Adding a step broke the steps after it

    Found

    Inserting the duplicate check changed what "the previous step" meant, so later fields came through empty.

    Fixed

    Pointed each field at the AI step by name, and now re-test the whole flow after every change.

How I built it

I used n8n's built-in AI assistant to generate the nodes. My work was designing the flow and the categories, choosing the test cases, reading the results critically, and directing each fix. I kept a build log of every problem and fix, which is where the findings above come from.

The workflow files, test data, screenshots and full build log are on GitHub. Anyone with n8n can import the workflows and run the same tests.

Next steps
  • Split "Unknown" into "Unknown size" and "Individual", since a student and a company of unclear size need different follow-ups.
  • Send a reminder when a draft has waited 24 hours without approval.
  • Add an error workflow so a failed run, such as an expired Google connection, sends an alert.
  • Run it on a real business's form and measure response time to hot leads and the share of drafts approved without edits.

The problem

A small private clinic gets every kind of message through one website form: bookings, price questions, complaints, people asking about symptoms, and occasionally someone describing an emergency. AI could help sort them, but in healthcare a wrong reply can do real harm.

I wanted to see how far AI could take the admin load off a fictional physio clinic, Hartfield Physiotherapy, while making sure it could never give medical advice or miss an emergency.

How it works

UrgentFixed 999 / NHS 111 safety message, sent immediately, plus an alert to the clinicFixed template

Safety design
  • AI never gives clinical advice. It classifies enquiries and drafts admin replies only.
  • When unsure, escalate. Possible emergency symptoms mean Urgent even when the person only asks to book. Any clinical question beats a booking request.
  • A keyword backstop. A simple rule checks for emergency terms like "chest pain" and "slurred", and forces Urgent whatever the AI decided.
  • Fixed text for anything safety-related. The urgent reply is the same approved wording every time, with the subject "Important: if this is an emergency, call 999".
  • An unanswered draft is never treated as handled. If nobody approves within 45 minutes, the enquiry is flagged for follow-up and an alert is sent.
  • Synthetic data only. Health information is special category data under UK GDPR, so every enquiry was made up.

What testing found

  • An unanswered booking would have looked handled

    Found

    By default, an approval that timed out was logged as "Draft rejected". A patient's booking request could sit unanswered with nothing flagging it.

    Fixed

    Added a separate "timed out, needs follow-up" status and an alert. Tested by shortening the wait to 2 minutes and letting it expire.

  • An accurate answer could still mislead

    Found

    Asked about sports massage and insurance, the draft said to check the policy covers "physiotherapy". Many policies cover physio but not massage.

    Fixed

    The AI matched the FAQ exactly, so the fix belongs in the FAQ, not the prompt. AI drafts are only as good as the content behind them.

  • The urgent reply was easy to miss

    Found

    The safety message had a calm, generic subject line, so someone skimming a busy inbox might not open it.

    Fixed

    Changed the subject to "Important: if this is an emergency, call 999", so the message works without being opened.

  • The backstop was never needed, which is fine

    Found

    The AI was cautious enough that the keyword check never had to overrule it in live tests.

    Kept

    It stays as a safety net for the day the AI gets it wrong. Its override was confirmed with a simulated AI answer.

How I built it

I used n8n's built-in AI assistant to generate the nodes. I designed the categories, the safety rules and the routing, wrote the test cases, checked every result against the sheet and the inbox, and directed each fix.

Next steps
  • Let the reviewer edit a draft before approving it, not only approve or reject.
  • Tighten the drafting prompt: answer only what was asked, and never confirm availability.
  • Add a data retention rule so enquiries are deleted after a set period.
  • Connect a real booking calendar so drafts can offer actual appointment times.
